# stockNavalon: how the bot works

stockNavalon is the bot that polyengine, the game engine in this repository, exists for. This document describes what's built. Plans and the reasoning behind them are in [`notes/design.md`](../notes/design.md), the checklist in [`notes/todo.md`](../notes/todo.md), and what training runs showed in [`notes/runs.md`](../notes/runs.md).

## 1. Overview

stockNavalon follows AlphaZero, despite the Stockfish-like name: Monte Carlo tree search guided by a neural network. Polytopia needs that: a turn is 10 to 30 commands, each chosen from about 60 legal ones, so a policy saying which moves are worth trying is essential.

| Piece | Where | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The network: one trunk with policy, value and belief heads | `python/stocknavalon/model.py` | 2 |
| The search: batched PUCT, across turn boundaries | `src/stockNavalon/search.h` | 3 |
| Fog: searching sampled versions of the hidden state | `src/stockNavalon/determinize.h` | 4 |
| Tribe inference: which tribes an unmet opponent could be | `src/stockNavalon/tribeInference.h` | [`tribeInference.md`](tribeInference.md) |
| The encoding: states, commands and targets as arrays | `src/stockNavalon/encoding.h` | [`encoding.md`](encoding.md) |
| Recording games and the replay buffer | `src/stockNavalon/record.h` | 5 |
| Self-play and training | `src/stockNavalon/selfPlay.h`, `python/stocknavalon/` | 5, 6 |

All of it reads the game only through `PlayerView` (what the player knows) and the engine's rules in `src/engine/rules.h`; nothing restates a rule.

## 2. The network

`model.py`: a 3×3 convolutional ResNet, 6 blocks of 64 channels by default (about 0.6M parameters).
- **Input** ([`encoding.md`](encoding.md)): about 100 planes over the padded N×N map (terrain, buildings, territory, cities, units, a threat map) and a global vector (stars, techs, tasks, diplomacy, the map type, and per opponent the score features and the tribes they could be), all relative to the player to move, so one network plays any seat. The global vector is added as a per-channel bias, and two blocks add global pooling (the board's mean and max).
- **Policy:** per-tile 1×1 convolutions for the per-tile command slots, plus a pooled branch for the global slots (research, diplomacy, End Turn). Illegal slots are masked out.
- **Value:** pooled features into a small MLP, tanh: the expected result for the player to move.
- **Side heads** ([`encoding.md`](encoding.md) section 8.5), never read by the search: the score lead 5 turns ahead and at the end (pooled, tanh), and each tile's owner when the game ends (per tile, 3 classes). They give the shared trunk many targets per position instead of one result per game, so the value learns from features like economy, armies and fronts, without the bot ever playing for score.
- **Belief heads** ([`encoding.md`](encoding.md) section 8): per opponent, their tribe, techs, stars (a mixture of negative binomials), where hidden units are and their types, how many, and how much they've explored. With fog, self-play and matches sample the hidden state from them (section 4).

## 3. The search

`search.h`: PUCT, `Q(s,a) + c · P(s,a) · √N(s) / (1 + N(s,a))` with `c` = 1.5.
- **First-play urgency** (as in Leela and KataGo): a move not tried yet counts as the node's value so far (its own evaluation and everything backed up through it), less `fpuReduction` (0.2) × √(the prior already explored), not as 0. Counted as 0, in a position that looks lost every untried move beat every tried one: with a gloomy network the policy's favourite got 4% of the visits instead of 30%.
- **`BatchedSearch`** runs many searches at once. Each decision gets `samples` determinized games, each with its own tree, and their root visits are added up. Every tree walks down until it needs an evaluation; `pending()` hands all waiting positions to the evaluator together, and `submit()` continues. `run(search, evaluator)` and `search(game, ...)` wrap the loop; `playTurn` plays a whole turn with it (the CLI bot).
- **Nodes** keep their `Game` and `LegalMoves`; a child's legal list comes from its parent's.
- **The horizon** is the number of turn changes a line may cross before it's scored without looking further (default 1: the opponent's reply is searched). Values are for the player to move and flip when backed up past a turn change; a finished game is ±1.
- **The move played** is the most visited; `SearchResult::choose` can sample in proportion to visits with a temperature. Self-play adds Dirichlet noise to the root priors.
- **Gumbel search at the root** (`SearchConfig::gumbel`, Danihelka et al. 2022; off by default, `--gumbel`): Gumbel noise on the root moves' logits picks `gumbelCandidates` (16, at most the simulations left) without replacement, the most likely ones without noise; sequential halving splits the budget evenly over the candidates and keeps the better half by noise + logit + σ(Q) until one is left, which is played (`SearchResult::chosen`). σ(Q) = (50 + the most visits of a root move) × 0.1 × Q, with unvisited moves' Q completed by the mixed value (the root's own value blended with the visited moves', weighted by prior) and rescaled to [0, 1] across the moves. The policy target is the improved policy softmax(logit + σ(Q)) over every root move (`SearchResult::policy`). With several determinized games each tree halves on its own, the noise the same per command across them, and the result pools them. Below the root, PUCT as above. In self-play the noise is on for full searches (it replaces the Dirichlet noise and the temperature) and, in the opening turns, quick ones; matches play without it. `searches.py` plays one network with each search against the other at equal simulations.
- **Evaluators:** the network (through Python, section 5), or `ScoreEvaluator` (uniform priors, the score lead squashed into [−1, 1]), which plays `play_stocknavalon` and bootstraps training.
- **Tree reuse within a turn** (`BatchedSearch::reuse`, `SelfPlayConfig::treeReuse`): after a move, the next decision continues from that move's subtree, its visits counting towards the new search's simulations, with root noise applied afresh. Only within the same player's turn, and only when the player's encoded view of each tree's new root is exactly the real position: with fog, a move that revealed something, or chance going differently, means a fresh search. With a trained network, 16–19% fewer evaluations per move, 19–24% more moves in the same time.

**Tests** (`tests/stockNavalon/search_test.cc`): it takes a win on the spot; one turn further, it sees an unstoppable capture as a loss on every move and finds the attack that prevents it; root visits and seeds behave. Gumbel search spends exactly its budget on its candidates, plays the most visited survivor, takes a win with its policy target on it, and continues reused trees.

## 4. Fog: determinization

The search can't run on the real `Game`: a scout's move would reveal real tiles behind the fog, and the opponent's replies would use their real units and stars. So each search runs on a sample of the hidden state consistent with the player's view (stage 1, plain determinization; [`notes/design.md`](../notes/design.md) has stage 2).

`determinize(real, player, beliefs, seed, detail)`:
1. **Copy** the real game.
2. **Wipe** everything the player doesn't know: unexplored tiles, units in the fog and hidden units, each opponent's stars, techs, tasks, kills, exploration and (until met) tribe, and the random-number state (always reseeded, so ruins and Explorer paths can't leak).
3. **Fill in**, in order:
   - **Unexplored tiles** from the network's `unexplored` belief head when the beliefs have it: for each tile its feature (village, ruin or nothing), then its terrain (a village's a field, a ruin's never shallow water), then a resource that fits the terrain. Villages and ruins too close to a settlement or each other are dropped, by the map generator's own rules (`resourceFitsTerrain`, `isCornerTile`, the spacing constants in `mapgen.h`); corners stay lighthouses. Without those beliefs (`priorBeliefs`), from a freshly generated map of the same type and tribes. Climate either way by the nearest capital.
   - **Enemy cities:** the capital where their starting score allows (`possibleCapitalTiles`), other fogged cities only where the player has seen their borders.
   - **The tribe,** if not met, from the possible set ([`tribeInference.md`](tribeInference.md)).
   - **Techs,** down the tech tree, with techs the player has proof of forced in: units that need them (beyond what conversions and ruins explain), buildings in their cities, a road the player didn't build on a map without starting roads, monuments, a hidden-unit warning. In 1v1 only; with more players these prove nothing.
   - **Hidden units:** in the fog, any type their techs allow on the terrain (the boat a land unit would be in at sea); on explored tiles (Cloaks, Dinghies), exactly matching the eye warnings the player sees.
4. **Repair** the engine's bookkeeping, renumbering units and cities by tile.
5. **Score check:** explored tiles (+5 each) are the slack. Their count is set so the sample matches the published score exactly (with the player's own effects since taken out); a sample that can't is rejected and redrawn. When the score is far above what exploring could cover, researchable techs are added first.

**Without fog** (`SearchConfig::fullInformation`, for training without fog first): the search runs one tree on the real game, its random state reseeded so ruins and Explorer paths stay chance, and the network's input is the full view ([`encoding.md`](encoding.md) section 10). The rules don't change: exploring still scores and units still walk to villages. Self-play mixes games with and without fog by `fog_share`.

`Detail::TILES_AND_UNITS` is enough within the player's own turn; `EVERYTHING` (the score check) is used when the search crosses into the opponent's turn.

**Beliefs:** in self-play and matches with `network_beliefs` (`--network-beliefs`, on by default), a network side's fogged decision first sends its position to the network once per turn for its belief outputs, decoded by `decodeBeliefs` ([`encoding.md`](encoding.md) section 8.4); every search that turn samples from them. Within a turn the opponent can't act, and beliefs about tiles the player can now see are ignored anyway. Elsewhere (the score evaluator, the CLI bots) `priorBeliefs(view)`: any possible tribe, a few techs, some stars, a few hidden units anywhere.

**Limits:** two players only; city connections and unit caps aren't repaired; about 60% of draws are rejected with the prior (mostly on turn 0); 60 to 250 µs a call.

**Tests** (`tests/stockNavalon/determinize_test.cc`), on random games of every map type: samples encode exactly like the real game from the player's side, match the published score, pass the engine's invariant checks and survive 100 random commands; and pairs of games differing only in hidden things give identical samples from the same seed (the leak test).

## 5. Self-play and records

- **A record** (`record.h`) is a game's setup and commands, not encoded positions: map settings, the game seed, every command, and the policy target for the moves it chose: the search's root visits, or with Gumbel search the improved policy in ten-thousandths (`POLICY_WEIGHT_SCALE`), both normalized when encoded. The engine is deterministic, so replaying rebuilds every position, and records outlive encoding changes. Text files: `save_records`, `load_records`.
- **Examples:** `encodeExamples(records, positions)` gives, for each (record, move), the position just before that move for the player to move: input, legal mask, policy target (the visits, normalized), value target, belief targets and masks, optionally through a symmetry of the map ([`encoding.md`](encoding.md) section 9).
- **The replay buffer** (`ReplayBuffer`, C++, thread-safe) holds recent games up to a number of positions, samples positions across them (`per_game` from each sampled game, which is replayed once for them all), and encodes minibatches in one locked step.
- **`SelfPlaySession`** (`selfPlay.h`) plays many games at once and hands every waiting position across them to the caller in one batch: `pending_inputs`, then `pending_slots` (the moves' slots, so only their logits leave the GPU), then `submit_move_logits`. A finished game's slot starts a new one at once. Each side can be the network, the score evaluator or the random bot, and games can come in seat-swapped pairs (for matches).
- **Peace treaties off** (`peace_treaties`, the game setting `GameSettings::peaceTreaties`; `--peace-treaties` to allow them): a bot that can't use treaties yet proposes, accepts and breaks them every few turns, and each break freezes its army and disbands its units inside the other's borders. The rules are otherwise the same, embassies included; records keep the setting so replays match.
- **Fog per game:** a `fog_share` of games are played with fog, the rest with full information (section 4). The record keeps which (`fog`), and training encodes its positions the same way.
- **Side-target data:** when a game ends, its final territory and each player's published score at every turn go into the record.
- **Final values:** for a game the turn limit cuts off, each seat's last root value from a network search is kept in the record, as the value target in place of a draw (section 6).
- **Playout cap randomization** (KataGo): a share of decisions (`full_share`) get the full search with root noise and are recorded as policy targets; the rest a quick search, only to play on. Visits-proportional choices for the first 3 turns, then the most visited.
- **When games stop:** the rules have no turn limit, so self-play stops a game at `max_turns` (150), or earlier at a **stalemate**: once anyone has attacked or captured, `stall_turns` (25) turns with no attack or capture by anyone. Quiet openings are left alone, since the clock starts at the first fight. Either way the game is truncated, not drawn (section 6), so the cutoff only decides when to stop collecting data; nothing in the network's input reveals it. `GameRecord.stalled` tells the two apart (not saved).

## 6. Training

`python/stocknavalon/` (PyTorch; `uv pip install -e ".[train]"`). `python -m stocknavalon.run --dir runs/NAME` runs it; Ctrl-C stops cleanly, and running again resumes.

**The learning rate** (`learning_rate` in `train.py`): `--lr` (2e-4) at its peak, a linear warm-up from 0 over `--lr-warmup-steps` (1,000) whenever the optimizer starts fresh (a new run, `--init`, new heads or a wider input, logged as "network grown"; a fresh Adam moves every weight by about the learning rate at once, which jolts a trained network), then a cosine down to `--lr-floor` (5e-5) over `--lr-decay-steps` (100,000) of the run, held there since training has no end and its data keeps changing. The status line shows it.

**Losses** (`train.py`): policy cross-entropy against the visits over legal slots (the KL divergence above the targets' entropy is reported too, since it shows learning where the cross-entropy can't); weighted squared error for the value; each belief head over its mask (stars by the negative log-likelihood of the mixture), all belief heads together weighted 0.25; squared error for the two score leads (weighted 0.25 together) and per-tile cross-entropy for the territory (0.5), each over its mask. Weights saved before the side heads existed load without them (`load_weights`), and the heads start fresh.

**The value target** is about winning (`GameRecord::value`):
- **A game decided by a capture:** +1 for the winner's positions, −1 for the loser's.
- **A game cut off by the turn limit or a stalemate** is truncated, not drawn. Each position takes its player's final value: the root value of the search at that seat's last decision with the network, stored in the record (`finalValues`). That's the network's own estimate of the player's chances where the game stopped.
- **No final value** (games from the score evaluator, whose values are score leads, and older records): 0.
- **Partly the position's own search value:** `(1 − s) × the above + s × root value`, the root value being the search's value of that position for its player (stored for every searched move, `RecordedMove::rootValue`), `s` from `--search-value-weight` (0.25). Every position of a decided game otherwise has the same ±1 target, turn 5 and turn 45 alike, which made the value overconfident and is easy to learn by heart; the search value is position-specific and looks ahead. The result still makes up most of the target, so real outcomes keep pulling. Score-evaluator searches store none.
- **A city term early in a run only:** `(1 − w) × that + w × city lead`, the city lead being (the player's cities − the best opponent's) ÷ their sum at the game's end (`GameRecord::cityValue`, from `finalCities`), with `w` from `--city-weight` (0.3) fading to 0 over `--city-fade-steps` (30,000) of the run's training steps. While true results are scarce, the final values are close to 0 and teach little; cities are what a capture takes, more directly tied to winning than score (which also rises with techs, exploring and units). It fades by steps alone, so nothing it affects can bring it back.
- **Against memorizing results:** every position of a game shares its result, so each is repeated hundreds of times. Positions of cut-off games weigh `--cut-off-weight` (0.3) in the value loss, decided games 1, and only `--value-share` (a tenth) of samples train the value at all. The value loss is averaged over the batch, not the weights, so a smaller weight is less pull.
- **The held-out set** is always encoded with pure win targets and every weight 1.

**The loop** (`run.py`):
1. **Bootstrap:** score-evaluator games until the buffer is full. A run started from given weights (`--init`) skips it: the network plays its own games from the start, and training waits for `--min-positions`.
2. **Then three threads, with no iterations:**
   - **Self-play** keeps `--parallel` games going in each of two sessions that take turns, so the CPU prepares one while the GPU evaluates the other. Each session advances its games and encodes their positions on its own pool of `--session-threads` (4) threads, kept waiting between batches (`ThreadPool`; threads started for every batch were slower than one); a game's moves don't depend on the thread count. Matches use the same count. It only ever plays the **best network** (`best.pt`), and adds finished games to the buffer and `records.txt`.
   - **Training** draws minibatches continuously, throttled to `--reuse` samples per position added, so it waits rather than overtrains. A new run first gets `--warmup-steps` on the bootstrap buffer. It keeps an exponential moving average of its weights (`--ema-decay` 0.999, BatchNorm statistics copied), which sits near the middle of where a fixed learning rate keeps the weights bouncing.
   - **Evaluation:**
     - **Gates,** on every multiple of `--gate-steps` (2,000) in training steps, so restarts don't shift them: the average plays the best network, `--gate-games` (128). It becomes the best, saved to `best.pt` and handed to self-play, only if it wins at least `--gate-threshold` (55%) of the games a capture decided, with at least `--gate-min-decided` (20) of them; games cut off by the turn limit count for neither side. Otherwise the best stays, so a bad stretch of training never reaches self-play.
     - Gates use the current fog share like self-play, so they measure what's being trained. A gate has to keep up with training: it plays all its games at once, to a shorter turn limit (`--gate-max-turns`, 75; captures come well before), stops as soon as its verdict can't change (`gate_settled`: the threshold out of reach, or already certain, whatever the games still going do), and has its own time limit (`--gate-max-minutes`, 30; games still going then count for neither side). Its progress lines (every 2 minutes) count decided games only. Status lines keep going during a gate (it runs alongside training and self-play, taking a share of the GPU), and pause only during anchor matches. A share of a gate's games then goes into the replay buffer (`--gate-games-trained`, a quarter), each thinned to self-play's `--full-share` of training positions (`thin_policy_targets`), so the gate's compute isn't all lost (not into the held-out set or self-play's measures). Unthinned, every move a full search with no root noise, they made up 60% of the training positions and sharpened the policy targets.
     - **Anchors,** every `--eval-steps`, run before any waiting gate (skipped at once while the best network hasn't changed), always with fog, sampling the hidden state from the network's beliefs only once fog is part of training (before that the belief heads are untrained, and the fixed priors are used): the best network (saved as `checkpoints/step_N.pt`) against the random bot, the score-evaluator search and the previous anchored network. A game at the turn limit goes to the side ahead on score; results split the captures into the network's and the opponent's.
     - Both use `--eval-games` in seat-swapped pairs with the same search and no root noise. Once 90% of a match is done the rest get 50 more commands, and past `--eval-max-minutes` they end at once.
   - A lock around each thread's GPU work (MPS has trouble with two threads submitting at once).
4. **Held-out loss:** one game in `--holdout-every` goes to `holdout.txt` instead of training; once there are 100 held-out games, 512 positions from them (one per game sampled, so across all of them) are encoded once and saved (`heldout.npz`, with which positions they were, by game seed and move), and every status line reports the losses on that fixed set, with pure win targets and every weight 1. When the encoding changes, the same positions are encoded again from `holdout.txt`, so the numbers stay comparable; only a set saved without its positions, or whose games are gone, is drawn again. The training value loss isn't comparable to it: a cut-off game weighs 0.3, only a share of samples counts, and the targets are blended with the city lead.

**Fog** (`fog.py`): training starts without fog and brings it in by a schedule, unless `--fog-share` fixes the share for the whole run.
- **No fog** until the bot is winning games: `--fog-trigger` (50%) of the latest 512 self-play games decided by a capture.
- **Then a ramp** by training steps, from no fog to all fog over `--fog-ramp-steps` (25,000), started once and never reversed. It mustn't follow a live measure of play, or it could loop (more fog, worse play, less fog), as the old score weight did.
- **A brake:** `--fog-brake` (3) failed gates in a row pause the ramp until a gate passes. Pausing can't oscillate the way reversing could.
- The schedule's state is in the checkpoint; the status line shows the current share, and the log when the ramp starts, pauses and resumes.

**Files** in the run directory: `records.txt` and `holdout.txt` (rewritten from the buffers at every checkpoint, so they stay the buffers' size), `checkpoint.pt` (network, its average, optimizer, step), `best.pt` (the network self-play plays), `checkpoints/`, `heldout.npz`, and `log.txt` (one JSON object a line; the terminal shows a readable version).

**The status line,** every `--log-seconds`: training steps a minute, ms a step, how long training waited; games a minute, positions a second; the share of the latest 512 games ended by a capture (and of the buffer), and by a stalemate; commands per turn over the latest 128 games (about 6 for an active network, under 3 for a passive one) and the step of the best network playing them; the reuse; the training and held-out losses. After the positions a second, where self-play's time went: the CPU (advancing games, encoding, handing results back), the GPU (running its batches and waiting for them) and the GPU lock (waiting while training or a gate uses the GPU). Mostly CPU means more cores would help; mostly GPU or lock, a faster GPU.

**Ratings** (`rating.py`): every gate and anchor result, decided games only, in one Bradley–Terry fit on the Elo scale with the random bot at 0 (one virtual draw per pairing keeps perfect records finite). After each gate and set of anchors the run logs the best network's rating; `python -m stocknavalon.rating runs/NAME` prints every best network's (`--all` for every network). Matches are played at different fog shares, so it's for the trend.

**Main options** (defaults): `--buffer` 500,000 positions (about 1,300 games), `--batch` 256, `--lr` 2e-4, `--reuse` 4, `--per-game` 1, `--augment` on (`--no-augment`), `--parallel` 64, `--simulations` 64, `--samples` 1, `--full-share` 0.5, `--quick-simulations` 16, `--horizon` 1, `--max-turns` 150, `--stall-turns` 25, the fog schedule (`--fog-share` to fix it instead), `--eval-steps` 5,000, `--eval-games` 64, `--eval-simulations` 50, `--gate-steps` 2,000, `--gate-games` 128. `measure.py` measures network and self-play speed.

**Inference precision** (`--inference-dtype`: float16 by default, float32 or bfloat16): self-play, gates and anchors run the network in it; training and the averaged weights stay float32. Against float32 on 512 real positions, float16 picked the same favourite move 99.8% of the time with values 0.0008 apart on average; bfloat16 99.2% and 0.008 (its wider range buys nothing here, its lower precision costs). `measure.py --dtypes ...` times each on the device. On MPS float16 doubled the network's speed (40,200 positions a second at batch 256 against 19,300) but self-play only by 10% (14,500 against 13,100 with 64 games a session): the CPU side (advancing games and encoding, one thread a session, transfers, Python) is now most of each batch's time. That was with one thread a session; `--session-threads` now spreads it.

**Playing a network:** `python -m stocknavalon.play runs/NAME lakes Imperius Bardur --size 11 --bot 1` (README). Its bot samples the hidden state from the network's beliefs, asked once a turn as in self-play (`--no-network-beliefs` for the fixed priors), and plays without peace treaties unless given `--peace-treaties`.
